Let's over time move clan traits to HEXACO and a few related
things such as verbal/numerical/spatial tilt.

At the moment, we want to model what clans are more likely
to generate "criminal" or "antisocial" members. The standard
assumptions would be:

*   Important
    *   Low H: entitlement, but also giving bad relatives a pass
    *   Low A: demanding, harsh
    *   Low E: but not all parts relevant
*   Less important
    *   High X: just because it creates more opportunities
    *   High O: nonconformity aspect
    *   Low C: low prudence

Also, we need to note that there's a separate "altruism"
subscore that's under H, E, and A rather than a single score.
This trait is important enough in our initial setting that
we might want to model the subscore too, but we'll see if
that's necessary.

I also notice that HAE is almost the key axis initially.
Perhaps in a small enough setting introversion and extraversion
don't look so different, people don't care too much if you're
nonconformist, and dependence isn't close enough for people
to care if you're hard-working. Perhaps then the other traits
become more important after certain social changes.

OK, so we have X, HAE, and AEC as existing traits. I guess one
point about altruism as a subscore is that we'd need it to have
Robin Hood, otherwise you can only be honest and generous or
thieving and cruel. This doesn't sound like the most important
thing in the world, but something about it appeals to my intuition,
and it seems there's long been the legend of the sympathetic
bandit. Perhaps they especially have a role to play in time of
breakdown, so let's try to keep this if we can.

Apparently we can factor out AE, which is not surprising, since
OCEAN combines them. The distinction could be important, though.
One way to think about is that that A is sign: happy vs angry,
while E is magnitude: emotional vs stoic.
